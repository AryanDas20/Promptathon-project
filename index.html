HOW VAULT WORKS (HIGH SCHOOL ARCHITECTURE GUIDE)

Distributed systems sound complex, but they are built on simple real-world ideas! Here is how Vault keeps your data 100% safe:

1. Object Sharding (Tearing the Book)

Instead of saving a huge 100GB video as a single massive file, Vault tears it into smaller 64MB chapters called Chunks. This allows multiple storage servers to download and read pieces simultaneously.

2. Quorum Replication (N=3 Safety Copies)

Every chunk is stored on 3 different computers across distinct data centers. Even if 2 computers catch fire or lose power, your data remains accessible from the 3rd replica.

3. Consistent Hash Ring (Clock Mapping)

Imagine a 360-degree clock. Every server gets a position on the clock. When a new file chunk is saved, its fingerprint maps to a number on the clock, placing it in the nearest server.

4. Bit-Rot Detection & Merkle Trees

Over time, physical computer hard drives age and flip random 0s and 1s (Bit-Rot). Vault checks SHA-256 digital fingerprints every few seconds to catch and repair silent corruption automatically.

5. Quorum Formula: R + W > N

R = nodes contacted on read, W = nodes contacted on write, N = total replicas. If R + W is greater than N, every read is guaranteed to overlap the latest write — that overlap is what makes the data "strongly consistent". Try it yourself in the Storage Calculator tab.

6. Erasure Coding (Cheaper Than N=3)

Instead of 3 full copies, split data into k data pieces plus m parity pieces (like a Sudoku checksum row). Any k of the k+m pieces can rebuild the original — far less storage overhead than full replication, at the cost of extra CPU work to rebuild.

7. Gossip Protocols & Membership

Nodes don't rely on one central "phone book". Instead, each node randomly tells a few peers what it knows ("gossips"), and that knowledge spreads through the whole cluster in O(log N) rounds — the same trick that makes a rumor spread through a school in a single day.

8. CAP Theorem

During a network split you can only fully keep two of three promises: Consistency, Availability, and Partition tolerance. Since real networks do split, Vault leans on Raft to stay strongly consistent for committed writes, while replication keeps it highly available for reads.

ABOUT THIS PROJECT

Distributed systems, made tangible

Vault is an interactive simulation of a fault-tolerant, distributed object storage system. It exists to make distributed-systems ideas tangible instead of abstract — sharding, quorum replication, consistent hashing, Raft consensus, and Merkle-tree anti-entropy — all running live in the browser.

Kill a node, partition an availability zone, or inject silent bit-rot from the Chaos Engineering tab, and watch the cluster detect the fault and repair itself in real time. The goal is simple: turn concepts that usually live in textbooks and whiteboards into something you can click, break, and watch heal.
